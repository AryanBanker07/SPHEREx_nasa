Markers & Dipole Detections
Step 01 • Blink & Invert

Watch the Sky Shift Every 6 Months

Press Play (Space) to blink through SPHEREx's 6-month all-sky passes plus the 2010 WISE baseline. Click the half-circle Invert icon (I) on the vertical toolbar — faint moving objects stand out sharply as black dots hopping across a white background.

Step 02 • Subtract Epochs to Reveal Dipoles

Use Difference Mode (D)

Click the overlapping-squares Difference icon (D). Stationary stars cancel to gray! Moving stars, brown dwarfs, and Planet X candidates appear as a paired cyan hole (where it was) + amber peak (where it moved). Variable stars appear as a single un-paired spot.

Step 03 • Fingerprint Molecules in 102 Bands

Click Any Object & Compare Detector Arrays

Select the Pointer tool (S) and click any object to plot its 0.75–5.0 µm spectrum. Switch the top Bands menu between Array 1 and Array 6 or RGB False-Color: freezing Y-dwarfs like WISE 0855 vanish at 1 µm and blaze crimson at 4.8 µm with a 3.05 µm water-ice trough!

Step 04 • Mark & Log Your Discovery

Tag Candidates (M) & Cross-Check Gaia

Toggle the Gaia DR3 icon (triangle) or switch to the WISE / Gaia Sky tab. If your moving object has no optical Gaia box, mark it with the crosshair tool (M) and click Done!

SPHEREx Field Guide

1. Cold Y-Dwarfs & Water Ice Clouds

Brown dwarfs cooler than 300 K emit almost zero visible or 1 µm light, peaking strongly in SPHEREx Array 6 (4.4–5.0 µm) with deep 3.05 µm H₂O ice and 3.3 µm CH₄ absorption.

2. Fast Stellar Proper Motion Dipoles

Nearby stars orbiting the Milky Way march steadily in a straight line across epochs. Subtracting surveys produces a crisp cyan-to-amber dipole aligned with their proper motion vector.

3. T-Dwarf Methane Binaries

T-type brown dwarfs (~1000 K) display unmistakable twin broad methane valleys at 1.67 µm (Array 2) and 3.30 µm (Array 4) alongside 4.7″/yr motion.

4. UXor Circumstellar Dust Occultations

Young stars surrounded by dusty accretion disks dim dramatically when dust streams cross our line of sight, while showing strong >2 µm warm dust excess in their 102-band SED.

5. Mira Pulsating Red Giants

Asymptotic Giant Branch stars expand and contract over hundreds of days, creating large brightness changes without positional shift and deep 4.67 µm CO absorption.
